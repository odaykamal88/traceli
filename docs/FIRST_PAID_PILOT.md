# Traceli first paid pilot playbook

Updated: 27 August 2026

This document converts the DIHAD conversations into a working ideal-customer profile, a contractable first offer, and a sales motion for the twelve follow-up calls beginning the week of 31 August 2026. It is an internal commercial hypothesis until the calls confirm account-specific facts.

## Commercial decision

Sell a **paid Award Assurance Pilot**, not a broad procurement platform and not a generic AI transformation.

The pilot promise is:

> Bring one completed tender. Traceli will reconstruct the rules-to-award evidence chain, expose where the decision record is incomplete, and test whether an independent reviewer can defend the award without rebuilding it from folders and email.

The software remains the durable product, but the first contract is a time-boxed assurance engagement delivered through a controlled Traceli workspace. This gives the customer a concrete outcome that can be funded from an audit, programme-assurance, internal-control, procurement-improvement, or technical-assistance budget.

## Evidence posture

**Known from the founder's DIHAD conversations:** twelve exhibitors accepted follow-up conversations, and the combination of restricted funding, overlapping rules, fragmented evidence, and consequences for weak procurement records was repeatedly recognizable.

**Known from DIHAD's published audience data:** the exhibition contains materially different organizations. The [DIHAD 2025 post-show report](https://dihad.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/DIHAD-2025-Post-Show-Report.pdf) describes exhibitor profiles as approximately 47% relief-aid suppliers, 41% UN/Red Cross or Red Crescent/NGO organizations, and 11% government agencies. This does not establish the composition of the twelve follow-ups.

**Missing:** organization type, contact role, buying authority, current systems, specific recent procurement, triggering consequence, budget source, security path, and contracting process for each follow-up.

The calls must turn the first-party signal into account-level evidence. Until then, do not treat all twelve as direct customers.

## Must-have positioning for the calls

Do not sell a nicer procurement workspace. Sell a control outcome tied to an existing obligation:

> Before the organization signs a material donor-funded award, can the accountable team prove the governing rule, supplier evidence, human judgment and required approval without rebuilding the story from its portal, ERP, files and email?

The category is **award assurance for donor-funded procurement**. The economic outcome is **protect funding eligibility and keep awards moving**. The product artifact is the **Traceli Award Assurance Pack**. This creates a repeatable control gate without claiming that Traceli certifies compliance or prevents fraud.

For each call, choose one documented consequence family that matches the account's trigger: audit finding, ineligible expenditure, funding behind remediation, or integrity sanction. Use only the controlled short wording and primary-source link in [PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md](./PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md). Then return to the prospect's own completed tender; the public case is urgency evidence, not the centre of the pitch.

The retrospective pilot is the paid wedge because it tests the organization's current proof gap safely. A successful second phase accompanies one live tender and places the readiness check before approval. That is the path from a one-off service to a recurring operational control.

## Ideal customer profile

### Primary direct customer

A humanitarian or development organization that:

- procures goods or services using donor-restricted, grant-funded, public, or otherwise ring-fenced money;
- must apply more than one rule set across donor conditions, internal policy, local law, and programme commitments;
- runs recurring, material procurements across countries, programmes, or operating teams;
- has an ERP, tender portal, shared drive, and email trail that record pieces of the process but not one defensible award narrative;
- experiences audit findings, complaint risk, no-objection or approval requirements, delayed closeout, or repeated evidence reconstruction;
- can name a completed tender suitable for a retrospective pilot and a senior owner willing to review the result.

Best-fit organization types are international and local NGOs, charities and foundations, Red Cross or Red Crescent entities, UN or intergovernmental programmes, government aid authorities, and prime implementers responsible for downstream partner procurement.

### Buying committee

| Role | Likely person | What they need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Champion | Head of procurement, procurement manager, grants/compliance lead | Less reconstruction, visible exceptions, a defensible recommendation |
| Economic buyer | Programme director, COO, CFO, country director, internal-audit or risk leader | Funding and institutional exposure reduced; a specific assurance outcome |
| Decision approver | Executive sponsor, programme owner, procurement committee owner | A bounded pilot, clear acceptance criteria, no disruption to the current system |
| Veto owners | Information security, legal/privacy, IT, records management | Controlled data boundary, retention terms, named subprocessors, exit/export |
| Independent validator | Internal audit, donor-assurance, M&E/MEAL or external reviewer | A source-linked record they can test without relying on Traceli's assertions |

M&E/MEAL is a useful stakeholder when procurement evidence affects programme assurance, but Traceli should not be positioned as an M&E platform.

### Trigger events

Prioritize accounts with one of these events already in motion:

- a recent audit finding or corrective-action plan;
- a new donor award or implementation agreement;
- an upcoming high-value or politically sensitive tender;
- a recent supplier complaint, challenged award, or integrity concern;
- an ERP or procurement-process transformation that leaves an assurance gap;
- programme closeout requiring dispersed procurement evidence;
- an overstretched procurement, grants, or audit team relying on manual reconstruction.

### Disqualifiers

Do not pursue a direct pilot now when the contact:

- is a pure relief-aid supplier seeking help to win tenders rather than an organization accountable for running procurement;
- recognizes the problem but cannot name a recent tender, consequence, or accountable owner;
- wants autonomous bid scoring or supplier selection;
- needs Traceli to replace its ERP, tender portal, or supplier marketplace;
- cannot identify a lawful, secure route for the pilot data;
- is interested only in an unpaid innovation exercise with no contract path.

A supplier may still be a referral source, integration partner, or future supplier-response user. That is a separate hypothesis and must not change the buyer-side roadmap before the first paid pilot.

## Twelve-call qualification score

Score each dimension from 0 to 2 immediately after the call.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Restricted-funding exposure | None | Occasional | Recurring/material |
| Rule complexity | One stable process | Some variation | Donor + internal + local rules |
| Evidence fragmentation | One controlled system | Some manual work | Multiple systems, files and email |
| Consequence or trigger | Abstract interest | General audit pressure | Named finding, deadline, complaint or tender |
| Accountable sponsor | No owner | Operational contact only | Champion plus economic-buyer route |
| Pilot readiness | No suitable case | Case exists, access uncertain | Completed tender and secure access path |
| Contract path | No budget or mechanism | Possible but unclear | Named budget owner and buying process |

- **11–14:** ask for the paid pilot decision and agree a contracting plan.
- **8–10:** close the single missing sponsor, trigger, data, or budget condition.
- **0–7:** nurture, seek a referral, or disqualify.

Never inflate a score to keep an account active. One contractable account is more valuable than twelve polite conversations.

## Current offer: Award Assurance Pilot

### Scope

- Six weeks from completion of the contractual and secure-data gate.
- One organization and one completed tender.
- One governing policy/donor pack and the tender/matrix versions used.
- Three to five submissions, two to four reviewers, and the material clarification and approval history where available.
- Retrospective analysis only; the customer's original decision remains authoritative.

### Deliverables

1. **Baseline and control map:** the applicable rules, required decision evidence, systems involved, and current reconstruction workflow.
2. **Source-linked award record:** requirements, criteria, supplier evidence, clarifications, judgments, exceptions, approvals, and chronology linked to their sources or explicitly marked missing.
3. **Independent-review session:** a reviewer who was not responsible for assembling the record attempts to answer why the supplier won.
4. **Pilot results brief:** measured reconstruction time, evidence gaps, control failures found, workflow implications, and a recommendation for a live or portfolio deployment.
5. **Customer export:** a human-readable decision pack and a machine-readable manifest when the minimum export boundary is ready.

### Success measures

Agree baselines during kickoff. Do not promise invented percentages.

- time required to reconstruct the award before and with Traceli;
- proportion of sampled material claims supported by an accepted source or an explicit unresolved gap;
- missing approvals, declarations, source versions, or clarifications surfaced;
- time required to produce the review pack;
- whether an independent reviewer can explain the recommendation from the resulting record;
- customer decision to run Traceli alongside one live tender or expand to another programme.

### Commercial hypothesis

- Founding-pilot fee: **USD 15,000 / AED 55,000**, fixed for the defined scope.
- Use the customer's required invoicing structure; do not imply that the price avoids its procurement rules.
- Credit the paid pilot fee against the first annual subscription if the customer signs the annual agreement within 60 days of the pilot readout.
- Scope expansion, document remediation, travel, translation, integration, or a live award workflow requires a change order.

The calls must validate willingness to pay and the budget source. Discount only in exchange for a smaller scope, a faster decision, reference rights that the customer can legally grant, or another explicit consideration. Do not trade away the paid nature of the validation.

### Explicit exclusions

- no autonomous compliance determination, supplier exclusion, scoring, or award recommendation;
- no replacement of the customer's ERP, tender portal, document repository, or audit function;
- no ingestion of sensitive documents before the NDA, classification, data-processing terms, approved transfer route, and technical security gate are complete;
- no claim that Traceli certifies legal or donor compliance;
- no production connector unless the signed scope names and funds it.

## Follow-up call structure

The objective of every call is a decision about a specific pilot path, not another general product reaction.

### Before the call

- Classify the account as buyer/implementer, potential channel, or supplier-only.
- Identify the contact's likely role and one plausible triggering event without presenting it as fact.
- Prepare the 44-second film and one requirement-to-evidence-to-approval journey.
- Select one governed public consequence example that matches the account's likely trigger; do not use more than one unless asked.
- Send or carry a one-page pilot scope; do not lead with the full roadmap.

### Thirty-minute agenda

1. **Reconnect — 3 minutes:** restate the specific DIHAD problem the contact recognized.
2. **Diagnose one completed award — 10 minutes:** identify the money, rules, systems, reconstruction work, and consequence.
3. **Show only the relevant proof path — 5 minutes:** rule to evidence to judgment to approval to export.
4. **Test the pilot — 7 minutes:** scope, data route, reviewer, success measure, budget owner, and contracting path.
5. **Close — 5 minutes:** agree the named tender, sponsor meeting, security/legal owner, proposal date, and decision date.

### Required discovery questions

1. “Which completed procurement from the last twelve months would be hardest to defend if a donor or auditor asked for the full reasoning next week?”
2. “What rule sets governed it, and where are the criteria, evidence, clarifications and approvals stored today?”
3. “How was the last audit, complaint or management review assembled, and who carried that work?”
4. “What happens operationally or financially when the evidence is incomplete or late?”
5. “Which system is authoritative for the award, and what material reasoning still sits outside it?”
6. “Who owns this exposure beyond procurement, and who would approve a six-week assurance pilot?”
7. “Which existing budget could fund a bounded pilot: programme assurance, audit remediation, internal controls, procurement improvement, or technical assistance?”
8. “Could one completed tender be made available under an NDA and an agreed data-classification route?”

### Closing ask

Use a conditional close:

> If we scope one completed tender, keep your existing process authoritative, and agree the data route before any files move, are you prepared to sponsor a paid six-week pilot at USD 15,000? If yes, who must approve the scope, security terms, and purchase this week?

Do not end with “I will send more information.” End with a dated mutual action: sponsor meeting, tender selection, security review, proposal review, or a clear no-decision.

## Objection posture

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| “Our ERP already has an audit log.” | “That log is one source. The pilot tests whether it can explain the governing rule, evidence, clarification, judgment and approval across every place they actually occurred.” |
| “There is no budget for procurement assurance software.” | “The pilot does not require a new platform programme. It produces one defined assurance artifact against a completed funded procurement. Let us identify whether the accountable owner already sits in programme assurance, audit remediation, internal controls, procurement improvement or technical assistance—and stop if none owns the consequence.” |
| “Internal audit already does this.” | “Audit should remain independent. Traceli helps the operating team maintain a testable record before audit and gives audit a faster record to inspect.” |
| “The documents are too sensitive.” | “The contract can be agreed now; no sensitive file enters Traceli until the NDA, classification, approved transfer route and technical gate pass.” |
| “The product is early.” | “That is why the first scope is retrospective, bounded and human-owned. The acceptance test is the record produced, not a claim of enterprise-wide automation.” |
| “Can this help us submit bids?” | “Not in this pilot. Traceli currently serves the organization accountable for running and defending the procurement. Supplier-response tooling would be a separate product decision.” |

## Minimum contract path

Within 24 hours of a qualified call, send a short proposal/SOW containing:

- named customer, sponsor, completed tender, and governing programme;
- the six-week scope, deliverables, exclusions, and acceptance review;
- customer responsibilities and weekly access to procurement and assurance owners;
- fee, invoicing, expiration date, and change-control terms;
- NDA, data classification, processing/subprocessor terms, region, retention, deletion, and exit/export;
- explicit statement that the customer's decision remains authoritative and Traceli does not certify compliance;
- kickoff conditions and a mutual action plan through signature.

## Conversion gate for the twelve calls

By the end of the follow-up cycle, success is:

- all twelve accounts classified and scored;
- at least four buyer-side accounts with a named completed tender and consequence;
- at least two sponsor or economic-buyer meetings;
- at least two written paid-pilot proposals;
- at least one signed pilot contract or a documented contracting path with named approvers and dates.

If the calls produce interest but no named tender, consequence, sponsor, or budget route, do not interpret that as validated demand. Refine the segment or offer before adding product scope.
