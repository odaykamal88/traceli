## D-UNFPA-01 — When the award explanation and minutes disagree

**Source U1:** UNFPA OAIS, [Audit of the UNFPA Country Office in the Kingdom of Morocco, IA/2024-16](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/audit-reports/2024-09-30_Audit_of_the_UNFPA_CO_in_Morocco_FINAL.pdf), 30 September 2024. Scope: calendar 2023; field mission 3–14 June 2024. Scope locator: printed p. 2, PDF p. 3, executive summary paragraphs 1–2.

| Claim ID / type | Claim or boundary | Exact U1 location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| U-01 / audit finding | A $27,120 award excluded an $11,400 bidder on technical grounds, while signed minutes recorded both bidders as qualifying for financial evaluation. | Printed p. 17 / PDF p. 18, paragraph 58(h) |
| U-02 / sample scope | This instance sat within 17 tested transactions worth $217,175. | Printed p. 17 / PDF p. 18, paragraph 58 introduction |
| U-03 / rule as cited by auditor | Under a two-envelope system, technical evaluation should be completed and signed before financial evaluation. | Printed p. 16 / PDF p. 17, paragraph 57(g) |
| U-04 / management response | Management agreed to recommendation 8; its plan included training and document review, targeting January 2025. | Printed pp. 17–18 / PDF pp. 18–19, recommendation 8 and action plan |

**Chronology:** 2023 transactions → June 2024 fieldwork → September 2024 final report → January 2025 planned completion. Later independent closure was not established in this run.

**Unresolved evidence:** The procurement file, actual technical scores and subsequent disposition are unavailable here. Price difference is not proven loss. This observation does not establish motive or which supplier should have won.

**Trace design inference:** A reviewer needs the disqualification rationale and the signed evaluation version side by side, plus a named disposition for the conflict. Merely attaching both files does not resolve their inconsistency. Proposed Trace fields: criterion/version → bid evidence → evaluation finding → signed minutes → award rationale → conflict owner/status. This is a design hypothesis, not a statement about current Trace-li capability.

**Candidate internal piece — “Which record explains the award?”**

The award explanation excluded a bidder; the signed minutes said it qualified for financial evaluation. UNFPA's Morocco audit records that contradiction, and management agreed to improve procurement controls. [U1, paragraph 58(h), recommendation 8](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/audit-reports/2024-09-30_Audit_of_the_UNFPA_CO_in_Morocco_FINAL.pdf#page=18)

The practical question is what the next reviewer can establish without calling the original team. Put the rationale, criteria and signed minutes in one review record. If they disagree, keep the exception open until a named person explains it. Start with one completed award: can a colleague follow why the selected supplier won?

## D-OCHA-01 — A completed project can still have an open assurance task

**Source O1:** UN OIOS, [Audit of OCHA operations in South Sudan, report 2024/052](https://oios.un.org/system/files/confidential-files/Reports/2024_052_pd.pdf), 31 October 2024. Audit period: 1 January 2022–15 May 2024; fieldwork March–July 2024. Scope locator: printed p. 2 / PDF p. 5, paragraphs 11–14.

| Claim ID / type | Claim or boundary | Exact location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O-01 / audit finding | Among 16 completed-2023 projects worth $23 million, 5/7 high-risk and 9/9 high-value medium-risk projects had at least one pending monitoring activity. | O1, printed p. 10 / PDF p. 13, paragraph 45(c) |
| O-02 / obligation | Monitoring was scheduled according to the Fund's operational modalities. | Same paragraph |
| O-03 / management explanation | OCHA cited logistical and staffing constraints. | Same paragraph |
| O-04 / management response | OCHA accepted the report's recommendations. | O1, executive summary, PDF p. 2 |
| O-05 / later aggregate status | A later OIOS table records five of six important recommendations implemented. | O2 below, printed/PDF p. 7, row 2024/052 |

**Source O2:** [OIOS recommendation implementation table, filename `rec_implementation_rate_iad_202602.pdf`](https://oios.un.org/sites/oios.un.org/files/Reports/rec_implementation_rate_iad_202602.pdf#page=7). Its title covers reports issued through December 2025. This aggregate row does not establish closure of the specific monitoring observation. It is not represented as the latest available status table.

**Unresolved evidence:** Project-level monitoring files, subsequent activity completion and outcomes are unavailable. Pending monitoring does not prove non-delivery. Recommendation 4, printed p. 11, does not expressly prescribe a monitoring remedy; do not equate its acceptance with resolution of paragraph 45(c).

**Trace design inference:** Keep project completion separate from assurance completion. Proposed chain: risk classification/version → required monitoring → scheduled activity → evidence of completion → exception/reason → owner/due date. If a visit becomes a remote check, retain the changed method and the human assessment of whether it meets the obligation. Do not claim the tool solves access or staffing constraints.

**Candidate internal piece — “The project finished. What remains to verify?”**

OCHA's South Sudan audit found pending monitoring on completed projects and recorded logistical and staffing constraints. Later reporting shows substantial progress on the audit recommendations. [O1, paragraph 45(c)](https://oios.un.org/system/files/confidential-files/Reports/2024_052_pd.pdf#page=13) · [O2, report row](https://oios.un.org/sites/oios.un.org/files/Reports/rec_implementation_rate_iad_202602.pdf#page=7)

For a reviewer, “completed” needs an object: the project, the visit, the evidence check or the exception. A useful handover keeps those states separate and shows who owns what remains. Take one project marked complete and ask which assurance tasks are still open, why, and what evidence would close them.

## D-CHEM-01 — Documents present, reconciliation unresolved

**Source C1:** [USAID OIG report 3-000-25-003-U, Ukraine: costs incurred by Chemonics](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/3-000-25-003-U.pdf). OIG transmittal: 21 February 2025; Premier Group results dated 23 January 2025. Audit period: 1 January 2018–31 December 2022; fieldwork 15 September 2023–30 September 2024. Locator: OIG memorandum PDF p. 2; auditor printed p. 2 / PDF p. 7. The independent audit firm's findings are transmitted by OIG, not a court determination.

| Claim ID / type | Claim or boundary | Exact C1 location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C-01 / audit finding | The auditor questioned $53,983.41 of direct costs for insufficient support permitting reconciliation. | Printed pp. 9–10 / PDF pp. 14–15, Finding 1 |
| C-02 / concrete mechanism | A $10,969.49 contractual/procurement item lacked an identifiable conversion rate for reconciliation. | Printed p. 9 / PDF p. 14, expense table |
| C-03 / disputed explanation | Chemonics said it supplied support, disputed the described system transition, and explained that D365 and ABACUS operated concurrently. | Printed p. 11 / PDF p. 16, Chemonics' response |
| C-04 / auditor's response | The auditor maintained the reconciliation finding after that response. | Printed pp. 11–12 / PDF pp. 16–17 |
| C-05 / material qualification | The conclusion reported no overbilling or underbilling; questioned costs are not established loss or recovery. | Printed p. 12 / PDF p. 17; OIG memorandum recommendation 1, PDF p. 4 |

**Rule/decision chain:** Finding 1 cites documentation and general-ledger reconciliation requirements under 2 CFR 200.302. This reports the audit criterion; it is not independent legal advice about applicability. The conversion evidence needed to follow C-02 is unresolved in the report, not proven never to have existed.

**Management and subsequent status:** Chemonics' response is included, even though OIG's transmittal says it issued without USAID management comments. These are different actors. [The OIG report page](https://oig.usaid.gov/node/7482), inspected on 14 September 2026, still displays four recommendations and no close dates; that absence alone is not proof of current non-compliance, debt or non-resolution. No recovery decision was located. Chemonics references supporting Annex A; it was not present in the inspected 31-page public PDF and has not been independently assessed here.

**Trace design inference:** A support file is one node. Reconciliation also needs the calculation, allocation basis, rate/date/source, ledger entry and reviewer disposition. Preserve a contested finding and the response together. Do not build this story around a failed system migration: the source itself contains a specific rebuttal.

**Candidate internal piece — “Can the next reviewer reproduce the amount?”**

The Chemonics Ukraine audit and company response disagree about the adequacy of supporting documents. The auditor retained the reconciliation finding. Chemonics also disputed a supposed system transition, explaining that its two systems operated together. [C1, Finding 1 and response](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/3-000-25-003-U.pdf#page=14)

A useful review record lets someone follow the amount from payment to allocation to ledger, including any exchange-rate calculation. It keeps the disagreement visible until resolved. Open one transaction: can a colleague reproduce its recorded amount from the linked support? That is the test; the existence of an attachment is only the beginning.

## D-DAI-01 — Before counting results, define who counts

**Source D1:** USAID OIG, [Audit of USAID/Indonesia's Urban Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Project, 5-497-15-002-P](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2018-06/5-497-15-002-p.pdf), 5 January 2015. Historical case involving Development Alternatives Inc. Audit scope: project inception through 31 March 2014; fieldwork 9 June–3 July 2014. Scope: printed pp. 12–13 / PDF pp. 15–16. This is not evidence of DAI's current operating condition.

| Claim ID / type | Claim or boundary | Exact location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-01 / audit finding | DAI staff were counted as training participants; monitoring staff had not verified entered data against source records. | D1, printed p. 8 / PDF p. 11, training-results section |
| D-02 / obligation | The audit cites USAID data-quality standards, including validity. | Same section; printed p. 9 / PDF p. 12 |
| D-03 / corrective response | USAID's evaluation records a revised annual result reducing the count by 3,894 after a separate sample review. | D1, printed p. 11 / PDF p. 14, recommendation 4; management details pp. 17–18 / PDF pp. 20–21 |
| D-04 / closure | Recommendations 4 and 5 show closure on 1 September 2015. | D2, recommendations 4–5 |

**Source D2:** [OIG report and recommendation record](https://oig.usaid.gov/node/623), inspected 14 September 2026. Closure of these recommendations must accompany any historical use of the case; it is not a certification of every subsequent result.

**Unresolved evidence and denominator discipline:** The original site sample was judgmental and not project-wide evidence. The audit's approximate 20% observation concerned the checked attendance records; the later 8.57% management correction concerned a different 4,500-person sample. Do not merge those figures or apply either to all project results. Underlying attendance and correction files are unavailable. The audit did not audit disbursements. This packet intentionally avoids a percentage headline.

**Trace design inference:** Proposed chain: indicator definition/version → eligible population → attendance record → exclusions → calculation → checked result → correction history → reviewer. Counting a person accurately still does not prove the intended outcome occurred. Keep activity counts distinct from outcome validation and from purchase intent in customer discovery.

**Candidate internal piece — “The attendance sheet is only the start of the result.”**

In a 2015 audit of an Indonesian water and sanitation project, the training count included DAI staff. USAID's response documented a correction; the relevant recommendations were later closed. [D1, training-results section and management evaluation](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2018-06/5-497-15-002-p.pdf#page=11) · [D2, recommendations 4–5](https://oig.usaid.gov/node/623)

The enduring review question is which people the indicator intended to count. A useful evidence trail preserves that definition with the attendance records, exclusions and corrected result. Before forwarding the next progress figure, ask a colleague to reproduce it from those records. Keep the correction visible: it is part of the record's value.
