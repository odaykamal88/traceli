## 1. UNFPA: Which record explains the award?

**Case:** D-UNFPA-01 · **Artifact:** HQ-D03-UNFPA-v1 · **Audience:** procurement and assurance reviewers inspecting an award rationale. **Distinct lesson:** linking two documents is insufficient when their recorded conclusions conflict. **CTA:** examine one completed award's rationale alongside the signed evaluation record. **Review:** pending copy review.

### Draft body

An award explanation should lead a reviewer back to the evaluation that supports it. What happens when the two records disagree?

In its September 2024 audit of UNFPA's Morocco office, the Office of Audit and Investigation Services described a $27,120 award that excluded an $11,400 bidder on technical grounds. The signed minutes, however, recorded both bidders as qualifying for financial evaluation. This was one observation within 17 tested transactions worth $217,175. The report also states that, under a two-envelope system, technical evaluation should be completed and signed before financial evaluation. [UNFPA audit, paragraphs 57(g) and 58(h)](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/audit-reports/2024-09-30_Audit_of_the_UNFPA_CO_in_Morocco_FINAL.pdf#page=17)

The public report does not give us the complete procurement file or technical scores. The price difference is not proven loss, and this observation does not establish motive or which supplier should have won. It establishes a question the recorded explanation leaves for the next reviewer.

Management agreed to recommendation 8 and planned training and document review, with January 2025 as the target. Later independent closure has not been established for this note. [Recommendation 8 and management action plan](https://www.unfpa.org/sites/default/files/audit-reports/2024-09-30_Audit_of_the_UNFPA_CO_in_Morocco_FINAL.pdf#page=18)

The useful review artifact is a visible connection between the criterion, the signed evaluation version and the award rationale. Where they disagree, the conflict needs an owner and a recorded disposition. Putting both documents in one folder leaves that work unfinished.

Take one completed award. Can a colleague follow the reason for selection, and see how any disagreement between the records was resolved?

## 2. OCHA: The project finished. What remains to verify?

**Case:** D-OCHA-01 · **Artifact:** HQ-D03-OCHA-v1 · **Audience:** fund, programme and assurance colleagues handing over completed projects. **Distinct lesson:** project completion and completion of its monitoring obligations need separate states. **CTA:** inspect the remaining assurance tasks for one completed project. **Review:** pending copy review; source review covers historical snapshots only.

### Draft body

A project can be marked complete while its monitoring record still has open work. The handover needs to say which of those things is finished.

The October 2024 OIOS audit of OCHA's operations in South Sudan examined 16 projects worth $23 million that had completed in 2023. At least one monitoring activity remained pending for five of seven high-risk projects and all nine high-value medium-risk projects. Those activities were scheduled under the Fund's operational modalities. OCHA cited logistical and staffing constraints. [OIOS report 2024/052, paragraph 45(c)](https://oios.un.org/system/files/confidential-files/Reports/2024_052_pd.pdf#page=13)

Pending monitoring does not establish that assistance was not delivered. The record identifies assurance work still outstanding at the time of the audit. An evidence tool would not, by itself, remove the access or staffing constraints described.

OCHA accepted the report's recommendations. A later OIOS table records five of six important recommendations as implemented. That aggregate progress does not establish closure of this specific monitoring observation, and the table is not presented here as the latest status. [OIOS implementation table, report 2024/052](https://oios.un.org/sites/oios.un.org/files/Reports/rec_implementation_rate_iad_202602.pdf#page=7)

For the next reviewer, a useful record connects the project's risk classification to the required check, its completion evidence and any open exception, with an owner and due date. Take one completed project: which assurance tasks remain, why, and what evidence would close them?

This draft relies on inspected historical source snapshots. Specific subsequent monitoring outcomes remain unknown. Independent live access to the linked sources was not confirmed and must be rechecked before release.

## 3. Chemonics: Can the next reviewer reproduce the amount?

**Case:** D-CHEM-01 · **Artifact:** HQ-D03-CHEMONICS-v1 · **Audience:** finance, procurement and assurance reviewers tracing a cost to its supporting calculation. **Distinct lesson:** a support document must connect to the amount, rate, allocation and ledger entry it is meant to explain. **CTA:** reproduce one recorded amount from its linked support. **Review:** pending copy review.

### Draft body

A supporting document has to let the next reviewer follow the calculation. Its presence alone may leave the recorded amount unresolved.

A USAID OIG report transmitted an independent audit firm's findings on costs incurred by Chemonics in Ukraine during 2018–2022. The auditor questioned $53,983.41 in direct costs because the support did not permit reconciliation. One $10,969.49 contractual/procurement item lacked an identifiable conversion rate for that reconciliation. [Report 3-000-25-003-U, Finding 1](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/3-000-25-003-U.pdf#page=14)

Chemonics disputed the finding. It said it had supplied supporting information and challenged the report's description of a system transition, explaining that D365 and ABACUS operated concurrently. The auditor maintained the reconciliation finding after considering that response. Both positions belong in the account. This is not a settled story about a failed system migration. [Company response and auditor's assessment](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/3-000-25-003-U.pdf#page=16)

The audit conclusion reported no overbilling or underbilling. Questioned costs are not established loss or a recovery decision. The supporting annex referenced by Chemonics was unavailable in the inspected public PDF, and a final allowability or recovery outcome has not been established for this note. The official recommendation page's absence of displayed close dates does not prove current non-resolution. [Conclusion](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/3-000-25-003-U.pdf#page=17) · [OIG recommendation record](https://oig.usaid.gov/node/7482)

The useful evidence chain runs from the payment through the calculation, exchange-rate basis and allocation to the ledger, with the reviewer's disposition attached. When a finding is contested, keep the response beside it.

Open one transaction. Can a colleague reproduce its recorded amount from the linked support, and identify the exact step that remains disputed?

## 4. DAI: Before counting results, define who counts

**Case:** D-DAI-01 · **Artifact:** HQ-D03-DAI-v1 · **Audience:** programme, monitoring and assurance reviewers checking a reported participation figure. **Distinct lesson:** an attendance record needs the beneficiary definition and exclusions before it supports a result count. **CTA:** reproduce one reported figure from definition to corrected result. **Review:** pending copy review.

### Draft body

An attendance sheet can accurately show who was in the room and still be insufficient to establish the number an indicator is supposed to report.

In a January 2015 audit of USAID/Indonesia's Urban Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Project, the OIG found that DAI staff were included in training-participant counts. Monitoring staff had not verified entered data against source records. The report discussed this against USAID's data-quality standards, including validity. This is a historical case, not evidence of DAI's current operating condition. [Audit 5-497-15-002-P, training-results section](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2018-06/5-497-15-002-p.pdf#page=11)

The response and correction matter. USAID's evaluation recorded a revised annual result that reduced the count by 3,894 following a separate sample review. The official record shows that the relevant recommendations, 4 and 5, were closed on 1 September 2015. [Management evaluation](https://oig.usaid.gov/sites/default/files/2018-06/5-497-15-002-p.pdf#page=14) · [Recommendation closure record](https://oig.usaid.gov/node/623)

The original audit sample was judgmental. The later correction used a different sample, so their percentages cannot be combined or applied to all project results. Underlying attendance and correction files were not available for this note. The audit did not audit disbursements, and a participation count alone does not validate the intended programme outcome.

For a reviewer, the chain begins with the indicator's definition: who qualifies, who is excluded and which source records support the count. It continues through the calculation, check and any correction. The revised figure should retain its history.

Before forwarding a progress figure, ask a colleague to reproduce it from that chain. Can they see who counted, why, and what changed after review?
