# Traceli ROI and value-case framework

Updated: 30 August 2026

This is the canonical internal framework for discussing Traceli's economic value in discovery, proposals, pilot measurement, marketing, and expansion decisions. It is a **Structural Case**, not a customer-specific ROI claim: account baselines and measured Traceli outcomes do not exist yet.

The framework incorporates useful hypotheses from an [external ROI and enterprise-positioning synthesis](https://grok.com/share/c2hhcmQtMw_a114da98-a62e-42a0-86a7-7c3f11e47135) reviewed on 27 August 2026. That synthesis is an input, not evidence. Its assumed prices, incident probabilities, loss ranges, risk-reduction percentages, and ROI outputs are not approved Traceli claims.

## Executive conclusion

Do not pitch Traceli as cheap insurance against a headline audit recovery. Pitch a measurable operating and assurance outcome:

> We will establish what one defensible award record costs your team today, then test whether Traceli reduces that work, shortens independent review, and makes unresolved exposure visible before the next live award.

The value case has three layers, in this order:

1. **Measured operating value:** staff and reviewer time required to find, reconstruct, inspect, and explain award evidence.
2. **Measured cycle value:** time and rework added by evidence requests, review queries, approvals, closeout, or no-objection steps where applicable.
3. **Customer-modelled risk value:** the probability and severity of findings, remediation, questioned expenditure, delayed funding, or other consequences—using the customer's own history and an explicitly agreed attribution assumption.

Operating value can be tested directly in the six-week pilot. Cycle value can be measured only when the pilot or a comparable live step reaches the same approval, review, no-objection, audit or closeout event; otherwise it remains a customer baseline and a pending expansion measure. The third layer should remain a scenario until customer evidence or longitudinal Traceli data supports it.

## Evidence posture

| Status | What belongs here today |
| --- | --- |
| **Known** | The founding six-week pilot is USD 15,000 / AED 55,000 for one completed tender. The pilot measures reconstruction and review performance and produces an Award Assurance Pack. Public primary sources demonstrate that procurement-control failures can have audit, eligibility, funding-continuity, and integrity consequences, but they do not establish an average Traceli customer's loss or Traceli's impact. |
| **Inferred** | Teams whose award evidence is spread across portals, ERP records, files, and email incur labour and review-cycle costs when reconstructing the decision. Existing assurance, internal-control, procurement-improvement, audit-remediation, programme-management, or technical-assistance work may provide an accountable outcome and owner. Each account must confirm this. |
| **Assumed** | No account-specific incident rate, financial exposure, loaded labour rate, award volume, time saving, risk-reduction percentage, annual price, or payback period is approved as a default assumption. Scenario assumptions must be named, editable, and customer-owned. |
| **Missing** | Baseline hours and people per award; review-query frequency and duration; annual material-award volume; external audit, consulting, and remediation spend; delay consequences; budget owner; permitted purchasing route; measured pilot change; and attributable risk mitigation. |

## Value buckets

Use the same five buckets in discovery and the readout. Do not force a bucket where the customer has no evidence.

| Value bucket | Traceli hypothesis | Pilot or customer evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Enhanced Productivity** | Less staff and reviewer time spent finding sources, reconstructing the award narrative, and answering repeated evidence questions. | People involved, baseline hours, loaded hourly cost, measured reconstruction and independent-review time. |
| **Cost Reduction** | Less external advisory, audit-support, remediation, and repeated review work attributable to fragmented award evidence. | Current invoices or internal cost baseline, attributable activity, and an agreed post-pilot comparison. |
| **Risk Reduction** | Earlier visibility of missing or ambiguous proof may reduce the likelihood or severity of documentation- and process-related consequences. | The customer's incident history, severity basis, applicable consequence, control owner, and a validated attribution factor. Do not default to 40%, 50%, or any other percentage. |
| **Revenue Acceleration** | Funding continuity or disbursement may improve only where the customer can show that award-proof delays or control conditions hold up funded activity. | A documented link between the procurement-control step and funding receipt, release, or continuation. This is not a default Traceli claim. |
| **Time to Market** | Faster evidence readiness and fewer review loops may help the organization approve awards and begin funded delivery sooner. | Baseline and measured days through the relevant review, no-objection, approval, or closeout step. |

## Customer ROI model

Use a transparent worksheet, not a universal ROI percentage.

```text
annual labour value
= material awards per year
× current reconstruction/review hours per award
× blended loaded hourly cost
× measured reduction attributable to Traceli

annual review-cycle labour value
= relevant reviews per year
× current extra-query hours per review
× blended loaded hourly cost
× measured reduction attributable to Traceli

annual external-cost value
= current attributable audit-support, consulting, or remediation spend
− expected attributable spend after adoption

risk-adjusted value
= customer-supported incident frequency
× customer-supported attributable severity
× validated mitigation factor

total quantified benefit
= labour value + review-cycle labour value + external-cost value + risk-adjusted value

ROI
= (total quantified benefit − Traceli investment) / Traceli investment
```

Keep operational value and risk-adjusted value visible as separate subtotals. A buyer should be able to remove the risk scenario and still see whether the measured operating case is credible.

Deduplicate overlapping staff time. For example, do not count reviewer follow-up hours in both `annual labour value` and `annual review-cycle labour value`.

### Pilot break-even view

The only current fixed input is the founding-pilot fee:

```text
break-even hours = USD 15,000 / customer-confirmed blended loaded hourly cost

break-even value per material award
= USD 15,000 / customer-confirmed annual material-award volume
```

Do not populate a labour rate or award volume without the customer. The purpose of this view is to make the buying threshold explicit, not to manufacture payback.

## Pilot measurement schedule

Record the baseline before showing the reconstructed Traceli result.

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot result | Evidence owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| People and staff hours needed to assemble the current award record | Customer-reported and, where possible, timestamped | Actual Traceli-assisted hours by role | Procurement champion |
| Time to locate and inspect evidence for sampled material requirements | Timed current-system task | Timed Traceli task on the same sample | Independent reviewer |
| Time to produce a review-ready pack | Current process or last comparable pack | Traceli export time and remaining manual work | Procurement champion |
| Follow-up questions needed to explain why the supplier won | Structured baseline review | Structured independent review | Independent reviewer |
| Material claims with an accepted source or explicit unresolved status | Current reconstructed sample | Traceli sample using the same denominator | Procurement and assurance owners |
| Missing approvals, declarations, versions, or clarifications surfaced | Current known state | Customer-accepted, rejected, or unresolved observations | Customer control owner |
| External support and remediation effort | Customer-provided historical baseline | Not usually measurable in six weeks; retain for later comparison | Finance, audit, or programme owner |
| Relevant review or approval cycle time | Customer-provided historical baseline | Measure only if the pilot or next live award reaches the same step | Programme owner |

The results brief must show denominators, time boundaries, people involved, exclusions, and customer dispositions. It must not convert a surfaced gap into an assumed avoided loss.

## Discovery for the next twelve calls

Ask enough to build a value hypothesis before presenting ROI:

1. Which completed award best represents the evidence burden you want to reduce?
2. When the full record was last requested, which people were involved and roughly how many hours or days did they spend?
3. How many material awards go through a comparable process each year?
4. How many follow-up questions or review cycles were required before the record was accepted, closed, or approved?
5. Did evidence friction require external audit support, consulting, remediation, or management escalation? What cost can be documented?
6. Did the delay affect approval, no-objection, disbursement, programme delivery, or closeout? What is the evidence for that link?
7. Which consequence does the executive sponsor actually own: staff capacity, award delay, audit remediation, funding eligibility, complaint response, or institutional risk?
8. Where do you currently budget for procurement compliance support, audit preparation, internal controls, programme assurance, fiduciary strengthening, or procurement improvement?
9. Who decides whether that budget and purchasing route may lawfully fund this pilot?
10. What measured result would make a live-tender or portfolio deployment an easy decision?

## Budget and procurement posture

Do not ask for a new `procurement assurance software` line if the buyer already funds the outcome. Explore programme or project management, programme assurance, internal controls, audit remediation, procurement improvement, fiduciary strengthening, and technical assistance as possible homes.

These are discovery routes, not eligibility claims. Traceli must never represent that its fee is automatically eligible under Incremental Operating Costs, Project Management Costs, a donor agreement, or any purchasing threshold. The customer's finance, procurement, legal, and programme owners must identify and approve the permitted accounting and contracting route.

## Approved pitch language

### First call

> I would not ask you to accept a generic ROI percentage. Let us take one completed award, establish the people, time, review loops, and consequence involved in defending it today, then use the six-week pilot to measure what changes.

### Executive sponsor

> The first return is operational: less time reconstructing the award and a faster independent answer to why the supplier won. The second is assurance: unresolved proof becomes visible before the next live award. Any financial-risk case should use your own history and assumptions, not a headline case from somewhere else.

### Human stewardship layer

> Traceli makes careful judgment visible, so the people responsible for an award can stand behind the decision and the public resources entrusted to them.

Use the stewardship line as supporting brand language. Do not tell a prospect that Traceli `restores dignity`, contrast the `cost of carelessness` with the `cost of care`, or imply moral failure. Traceli respects professional judgment by making it legible; it does not judge the worth of the people or institution doing the work.

## Public evidence and ROI guardrails

- Public failure cases establish that the consequence category exists; they do not provide the prospect's probability, expected loss, or Traceli attribution.
- Never say Traceli would have prevented a cited event, that one avoided finding pays for the product, or that the product is insurance against a catastrophic outcome.
- Never generalize the amount in one official case into a `typical` finding cost without a representative dataset and reviewed methodology.
- Never publish a risk-reduction percentage or payback period before Traceli has measured evidence.
- Keep exact source status—observation, finding, recommendation, recovery, sanction, or suspension—intact under [PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md](./PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md).
- If a customer supplies a scenario, label its inputs and outputs as customer-specific and do not reuse them as a Traceli benchmark without permission and sufficient comparability.

## Pricing and expansion

Keep the founding pilot at USD 15,000 / AED 55,000 for the defined scope. Do not adopt speculative annual project or enterprise price bands from the external synthesis.

Price an annual expansion only after the pilot clarifies material-award volume, users and roles, workflow and reviewer scope, security and data requirements, integrations, service effort, and measured value. The existing offer may credit the paid pilot fee against the first annual subscription signed within 60 days of the readout; the annual price remains a separate commercial decision.

At readout, recommend one of three explicit outcomes:

1. **Stop:** the proof burden, measurable value, or accountable owner is insufficient.
2. **Repeat:** another completed tender is needed to test whether the result generalizes.
3. **Expand:** accompany one live tender or a defined award portfolio, with an agreed measurement plan and customer-specific business case.
