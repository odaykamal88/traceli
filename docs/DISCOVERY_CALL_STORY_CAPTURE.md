# Tender story prompts for discovery calls

Updated: 28 August 2026

Use this as a short reference during Traceli discovery and follow-up calls. The commercial objective remains a named tender, sponsor, budget route and next action. These prompts add a separate learning lane for real tender stories that may sharpen the product and, with permission, future publications.

## Ask naturally

Do not run this as an interview script. Use two or three prompts when the contact mentions a real procurement problem.

- “Can you tell me about a tender where the decision itself may have been reasonable, but proving how it was reached became difficult?”
- “When was the full award record last requested by a donor, auditor, complainant or senior manager?”
- “What took the longest to find or explain?”
- “Which part of the reasoning lived outside the formal system, perhaps in email, a spreadsheet, meeting notes or somebody's memory?”
- “Did the issue become visible before the award was signed or only afterwards?”
- “What happened next? Was it extra review, delayed approval, a finding, remediation, questioned expenditure, a complaint or something else?”
- “Who had to carry the response, and what other work stopped while they reconstructed the record?”
- “What would you have wanted to see in one place before the committee or signatory approved the award?”
- “Is there a lesson from that tender that other procurement teams should hear?”

## Permission question

Ask only after the person has finished the story:

> “This is useful for understanding the problem. May I keep it as private product research? Separately, would you ever be comfortable with the pattern being used anonymously, subject to your review?”

Record one permission level:

- private product research only;
- anonymized pattern, wording requires review;
- attributable quotation, wording requires review;
- no permission recorded, do not publish.

Silence is not permission.

## Clarify without leading

If the story may be useful, ask for:

- the approximate year and procurement type;
- whether it was completed or still active;
- the relevant donor or rule family, if disclosure is permitted;
- the missing or disputed artifact;
- the exact consequence and whether it was documented;
- whether a public audit, decision or other source exists;
- whether another participant could corroborate the account;
- which details would identify the organization or suppliers.

Do not suggest a consequence and ask the contact to agree. Do not ask for confidential bid material, supplier names, protected personal data or active evaluation information.
