# Traceli publication and PR strategy

Updated: 28 August 2026

This plan turns Traceli's existing public-source evidence into a credible demand campaign for award assurance. It is designed to make procurement officers, grants and donor-relations teams, donors, executives, and assurance leaders recognize a specific institutional risk and adopt a pre-award proof gate.

It does not replace the claim controls in [BRAND_SYSTEM.md](./BRAND_SYSTEM.md), the approved wording in [PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md](./PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md), or the value boundaries in [ROI_VALUE_CASE.md](./ROI_VALUE_CASE.md).

## Executive decision

Build the campaign around one proposition:

> The award is not ready when the evaluation ends. It is ready when the accountable team can prove the rule, evidence, human judgment, exception and approval behind the decision.

The editorial platform is **Before You Sign: The Award Proof Gap**.

The campaign should create urgency through documented consequences, not generalized fear. Every asset moves through the same sequence:

1. An official record documents a control or decision-trace failure.
2. The record states a precise audit, financial, funding, delay or integrity consequence.
3. The reader is asked whether the same proof could be produced from one of their awards today.
4. A vendor-neutral award-assurance control is presented as the institutional response.
5. Traceli is shown as the fastest bounded way to test and operate that control.

The strategic aim is not to make a donor mandate a named vendor. That can create neutrality, competition and sole-source objections. The aim is to make donors and executives mandate the **outcome**—a source-linked award-assurance record before material award approval—then make Traceli the clearest reference implementation.

## Why this message is timely

Aid is scarcer and fiduciary tolerance is tightening. The OECD reports that official development assistance fell 23.1% in real terms in 2025, the largest annual decline on record, and projects another decline in 2026. The defensible message is therefore not “procurement teams are careless.” It is:

> When every aid dollar is under more pressure, no organization can afford to discover after sign-off that the award record cannot explain the decision.

This macro context is supported by the [OECD's preliminary 2025 aid data](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2026/04/international-aid-fell-sharply-in-2025-says-oecd.html). OLAF also reports a rise in complex procurement irregularity and fraud investigations, including conflicts of interest, in grants and administrative expenditure; this supports the relevance of the control category without establishing prevalence among Traceli's buyers. See [OLAF's 2025 trends and figures](https://ec.europa.eu/olaf-report/2025/trends-and-figures_en.html).

## The mandate path

Awareness alone will not put Traceli into an award allocation. The campaign must manufacture five artifacts that a buyer can reuse inside an institution:

| Buyer step | Artifact Traceli publishes | Institutional action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize the consequence | Official-record case study | “This risk exists and reaches my remit.” |
| Diagnose the local exposure | Award Proof Readiness Checklist | “We cannot answer all of these questions from one record.” |
| Define the control | Model Award-Assurance Clause | “This should be required before approval.” |
| Secure sponsorship and budget | Executive decision memo and pilot value sheet | “There is an owner, outcome and lawful budget route.” |
| Test it safely | Six-week retrospective pilot brief | “Use one completed tender before changing live policy.” |

The campaign CTA should progress accordingly:

`Inspect the official record` → `Test one award` → `Adopt the control` → `Bring one completed tender`

Do not send cold readers directly from a headline amount to “book a demo.” The diagnostic and control artifacts make the conversion feel like governance work rather than software promotion.

## Audience tension map

| Audience | Legitimate concern to activate | Proof they need | Action to request |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Procurement officers | A decision they handled may later be reconstructed from scattered files under time pressure | Evaluation chronology, source-linked scoring, clarifications, conflicts, approvals | Baseline one completed tender |
| Grants, compliance and donor relations | A procurement defect can become an eligibility, remediation or reporting problem | Donor condition mapped to the actual award record | Sponsor a cross-functional assurance review |
| Donors and fund managers | Implementer reporting may show that a procedure occurred without making the award reasoning independently testable | A reusable, vendor-neutral control and review artifact | Add an award-assurance deliverable to grant or implementation controls |
| CFO, COO, programme and country executives | The financial and reputational consequence leaves procurement and lands with the signatory | A concise exception and approval view with named ownership | Make award readiness a sign-off condition |
| Internal audit, risk and assurance | The operating team may not maintain a record that can be tested without reconstruction | Source, actor, timestamp, disposition and preserved chronology | Validate the control while retaining audit independence |
| IT, security and records | A new control can create another uncontrolled document store | Bounded architecture, authoritative-system boundary, retention and export controls | Approve a retrospective data gate |

## Editorial platform

### Series identity

**Name:** Before You Sign: The Award Proof Gap

**Category line:** Award assurance for donor-funded procurement

**Recurring question:** Could your team prove this award before signing it?

**Primary CTA:** Test one completed tender

**Quiet brand line:** Make careful judgment visible

### Editorial formula

Every case study should use the controlled structure already defined in the evidence register, expanded with two conversion elements:

1. **What the official record says** — issuer, date, procedural status and direct source.
2. **What failed** — the documented control, evidence or decision-trace issue.
3. **What followed** — only the documented consequence.
4. **What a signatory would need to see** — the proof bundle that should exist before approval.
5. **The award-assurance question** — one question the reader can apply locally.
6. **Where Traceli fits** — the control Traceli is designed to support, without a prevention claim.
7. **Boundary** — no customer relationship, endorsement, certification or claim that Traceli would have changed the event.
8. **Next action** — inspect the checklist or bring one completed tender.

### Emotional posture

Use consequence, accountability and professional self-recognition. Avoid humiliation, accusation and catastrophe theatre.

Good:

> The evaluation was complete. The record was not. Years later, the institution still had to explain what the file could not.

Avoid:

> Your team could lose millions because its procurement is broken.

The official institution is not the villain. The villain is the missing connection between rule, evidence, judgment and approval. This keeps Traceli credible with the same institutions it wants as customers and partners.

### Channel calls

**LinkedIn before X.** The buyer is identifiable by role, organization and sector, and the content benefits from professional context. Founder-led posts should carry the point of view; the company page should archive evidence and product proof. Use selective paid amplification only after an organic post earns comments or saves from the intended roles.

**IAPG and Humentum before Product Hunt.** A single closed discussion with procurement directors, finance leaders or donor-policy practitioners can change the control language used by institutions. Product Hunt's own guide describes its community as makers, technologists, product people, entrepreneurs, investors and early adopters. That is useful for product legitimacy, but it is not the primary buying committee for donor-funded procurement. See the [Product Hunt launch guide](https://www.producthunt.com/launch).

**Earned Devex before sponsored Devex.** Pitch an evidence-led opinion or data story first. If paid content is used, keep the sponsorship label explicit and buy it for reach, not borrowed editorial credibility. Devex distinguishes opinion, independent news and sponsored content in its published ethics standard.

**Partnership before promotion in governance communities.** Open Contracting Partnership, audit bodies and donor-policy forums should receive methods, templates and evidence—not a disguised product advert. The institutional relationship is more valuable than one backlink.

## Conversion assets

### Award Proof Readiness Checklist

The checklist should ask whether one award can show, from one governed record:

1. the exact rule and version that governed each material criterion;
2. the supplier source behind each material evaluation claim;
3. the named reviewer's judgment, timestamp and rationale;
4. every clarification and its effect on the evaluation;
5. conflict, confidentiality and eligibility declarations;
6. each exception, owner, basis and approval;
7. the final authority, approval sequence and signed chronology.

Scoring is for conversation, not certification. Label results `evidence visible`, `evidence dispersed`, `unresolved` or `not tested`; do not label an organization compliant or non-compliant.

### Draft model award-assurance clause

This is campaign language for review by donor, legal, procurement and audit practitioners. It is not legal advice or a claim that any named donor already requires Traceli.

> Before approval of a material procurement award, the implementing organization shall maintain a reviewable award-assurance record identifying the governing requirements and versions, evaluation criteria, material supplier evidence, clarifications, evaluator judgments, conflict and confidentiality declarations, exceptions, approvals and decision chronology. Each material award claim shall be linked to an accepted source or recorded as an explicit unresolved item or approved exception with a named owner and disposition. The record shall remain exportable and independently reviewable in accordance with the agreement's access, retention, data-protection and audit provisions. The implementing organization's authorized decision-makers remain responsible for the award.

The clause mandates the evidence outcome, not the technology. Traceli can then be positioned as the governed workspace and assurance pack that makes the requirement practical.

### Executive decision memo

One page, written for forwarding:

- the consequence the executive owns;
- the one-award diagnostic result;
- the control being proposed;
- what current systems do and do not evidence;
- the six-week retrospective pilot boundary;
- fee, data gate and acceptance measures;
- explicit stop, repeat or expand decision at readout.

## PR moments worth pitching

Do not issue a press release for each public failure case. The following moments can justify earned media:

1. **A reviewed 12-case institutional record study** with transparent selection criteria and no prevalence claim.
2. **Publication of a practitioner-reviewed Model Award-Assurance Clause** with donor, procurement and audit contributors.
3. **The first permissioned pilot result** showing measured reconstruction time, independent-review performance and remaining gaps.
4. **A partnership or working group** with a credible procurement, finance, governance or donor network.
5. **A material product release** that lets an organization produce and export the Award Assurance Pack from a real bounded workflow.

The headline should be the new evidence or institutional action, not “Traceli announces thought leadership.”

## Claim, legal and reputation controls

Before publication, every case needs:

- the issuing institution's primary source and a stable archived copy or citation record;
- the exact procedural status: observation, finding, recommendation, recovery, suspension, judgment, settlement or debarment;
- amount, date, geography and actor checked twice;
- a controlled paraphrase and a named factual reviewer;
- legal/comms approval for the public version;
- a visible statement that the institution is not a Traceli customer or endorser;
- no statement that Traceli would have prevented the event;
- no conversion of a case amount into a prospect's expected loss or Traceli ROI;
- a correction and archive process if the source or procedural status changes.

Avoid overusing the word `corruption`. Most of the strongest stories are about inadequate evidence, late oversight, missing declarations, inconsistent evaluation and unresolved exceptions. Precise language makes the campaign more credible and more recognizable.

## Resource allocation

For the first twelve weeks, allocate campaign effort approximately as follows:

- **35% evidence and claim review:** source research, factual review, legal/comms review and register maintenance;
- **25% practitioner distribution:** IAPG, Humentum, DIHAD follow-up, Bond and direct donor/partner briefings;
- **20% canonical assets:** case pages, checklist, model clause, executive memo and email capture;
- **15% repurposing and amplification:** LinkedIn documents, founder video, email and targeted paid distribution;
- **5% startup and mass-social channels:** Product Hunt preparation, X and opportunistic founder-community activity.

The allocation should move toward customer evidence after the first pilot. A permissioned measured customer case is more valuable than another ten public failure summaries.

## Strategic summary

Traceli should own a narrow, memorable idea:

> A donor-funded award is not ready to sign until its proof is ready to review.

Public failure records create the urgency. The readiness checklist creates self-recognition. The model clause creates the mandate. The retrospective pilot creates a safe buying action. The Award Assurance Pack makes the control repeatable.

That sequence is the route from “interesting procurement technology” to an institutional requirement.
