# Traceli brand and product-expression system

Updated: 30 August 2026

This is the canonical reference for Traceli's public story, visual language, product iconography, affiliation treatment, and demonstration assets. New screens and marketing surfaces should extend this system rather than inventing a parallel one.

For LinkedIn company images, carousels and captions, apply the [LinkedIn visual and voice guide](editorial/LINKEDIN_VISUAL_AND_VOICE_GUIDE.md), adopted on 9 September 2026 at Odey's request. This channel uses bold sans-serif hooks, prominent editorial imagery and selective yellow text highlights while retaining Traceli's identity and evidence standards.

## Category and promise

Category:

> Award assurance for donor-funded procurement.

Primary statement:

> Prove the award before you sign it.

Supporting line:

> Traceli gives the accountable team one source-linked record of the governing rule, supplier evidence, human judgment and required approval—before a donor review, no-objection, complaint or audit forces the reconstruction.

Traceli should be understood in ten seconds as the pre-award control gate that protects funding eligibility and keeps awards moving. It sits beside the tender portal and ERP; it does not compete to become either system of record. Its named output is the **Traceli Award Assurance Pack**: the source-linked record of the rules, evidence, judgments, exceptions, approvals and chronology behind an award.

The first useful demonstration must answer one question: **why did this supplier win?** The stronger commercial question is: **can the accountable team prove that answer before it signs?**

Internal operating principle:

> No proof, no award readiness.

Do not publish the shortened phrase `No proof, no award` as an absolute compliance promise. In public language, `ready` means that the buyer's configured control profile is complete or has named, approved exceptions. It does not mean certified compliant, legally approved, fraud-free, donor-approved or guaranteed to pass an audit.

Use plain procurement outcomes before technical mechanisms. Prefer `Map the award controls`, `Surface the evidence gaps`, and `Issue the assurance pack` over a feature catalogue. `Surface` means make the evidence status visible for human disposition; the retrospective pilot does not remediate source documents or make a compliance determination. Do not describe Traceli as autonomous decision-making, an AI scoring engine, another tender portal, an auditor or a compliance certification.

## Must-have narrative

The message should connect a procurement control failure to an executive consequence without fearmongering:

1. A material award claim is not connected to its rule, evidence, judgment or approval.
2. The gap survives sign-off because the proof is distributed across portals, ERP records, shared files and email.
3. The gap becomes expensive when a donor, auditor, complainant or investigator asks the organization to reconstruct the decision.
4. The consequence can be delay, an audit finding, ineligible expenditure, remediation conditions, sanction exposure or loss of trust.
5. Traceli moves the proof test before sign-off and preserves the answer in one governed record.

Lead with the controllable executive outcome: **protect funding eligibility and keep awards moving.** Use specific consequence examples only when an approved primary source supports the claim. Never imply that every documentation gap causes funding suspension or that Traceli prevents corruption.

## Stewardship and professional dignity

The emotional centre of the brand is stewardship: public and donor-restricted resources are held in trust, and the people responsible for an award deserve a record that makes their careful judgment legible.

Approved supporting ideas:

- `Make careful judgment visible.`
- `A decision your team can stand behind.`
- `Treat the award record with the care the public resources deserve.`
- `Make the rules, evidence and human judgment clear enough to defend years later.`

This is a supporting layer, not the primary category statement. Keep it quiet, specific, and respectful. Do not claim that Traceli `restores dignity`, contrast a prospect's `carelessness` with Traceli's `care`, aestheticize compliance as moral purity, or imply that weak records make the people involved unworthy. The product supports professional judgment; it does not replace or morally grade it.

## Value and ROI expression

Traceli's public value story follows an evidence ladder:

1. Show the fragmented work the buyer recognizes.
2. Show the measurable operating result the pilot tests.
3. Show the governed Award Assurance Pack and remaining gaps.
4. Discuss financial-risk scenarios only with customer-confirmed inputs.

Present measured facts, customer inputs, scenario assumptions, and public consequence examples as visually distinct evidence types. Never place a headline recovery amount beside the pilot price in a way that implies causation or guaranteed avoidance. Do not publish a generic ROI percentage, payback period, `typical finding cost`, `one finding pays`, or `cheap insurance` claim without an approved methodology and evidence under [ROI_VALUE_CASE.md](./ROI_VALUE_CASE.md).

## The 10/44/90 interaction rule

- The first viewport earns attention in ten seconds: one promise, one supporting sentence, one dominant pilot action, and one secondary proof-film action.
- The 44-second film shows one deterministic tender-to-proof story. The deeper workspace remains available for a guided ninety-second journey after the buyer names a relevant tender or proof gap.
- Avoid carousels, competing dashboards, or explanations that duplicate the product visual.
- Start the walkthrough from a clean tender workspace. Show information appearing only when the corresponding action occurs.
- End with a low-friction pilot request: `Test one completed tender.` The retrospective pilot tests whether the organization can reconstruct an accepted Award Assurance Pack before Traceli accompanies a live award.
- Label fictional, staged, sanitized, and not-yet-live behavior explicitly.

## Visual hierarchy

Traceli borrows the discipline—not the identity—of mature enterprise platforms such as WorkOS and Vanta:

1. Quiet external affiliation marks.
2. Strong proprietary product identity.
3. Product surfaces that demonstrate the claim.
4. Restrained copy and generous negative space.

The public page uses a dark assurance field around the promise and an ivory operational field for product explanations. Dense application screens may use more neutral surfaces, but the same hierarchy and tokens apply.

Mint signals assurance or linked evidence; it is not general decoration. Red and amber are reserved for genuine risk, exception, or blocked states.

## Typography

- **IBM Plex Sans** is the interface and body face. It should feel operational and legible.
- **Newsreader** is the display face for the primary promise and a small number of major section statements.
- **IBM Plex Mono** is reserved for identifiers, sequence labels, evidence metadata, and compact status language.
- Avoid using display type for dense application controls or mono type for long prose.
- Headlines should be short, with tight tracking and deliberate line breaks. Supporting copy should remain readable at normal interface sizes.

## Traceli mark and wordmark

The Traceli emblem is a trace-node system: a layered diamond field, connected waypoints, and one mint terminal node. It represents a source-linked decision path rather than a generic security shield.

- Keep the emblem and `TRACELI` wordmark together in primary navigation and formal brand contexts.
- Use ivory for the wordmark on navy. Preserve the mint terminal node.
- Maintain clear space of at least half the emblem width around the lockup.
- Do not substitute generic shields, locks, sparkles, brains, robots, or model-provider marks.
- Do not stretch, rotate, recolor, add slogans inside, or place the mark on visually noisy imagery.

The current in-product lockup is canonical for the pilot. A future professionally drawn vector may refine its geometry, but it must preserve the trace-node concept and remain recognizably continuous with this version.

## Product icons

External brands recede; Traceli product capabilities receive the richer visual treatment.

- Use one semantic line icon from the existing icon library as the primary glyph.
- Place it in a layered tile with a restrained grid, soft directional gradient, and one small secondary status glyph.
- Keep line weight and corner language consistent across a set.
- Use mint for rule/evidence preparation, blue for evaluation/inspection, and violet for assurance/locked proof.
- Every icon must support a named workflow outcome. Avoid decorative icon proliferation.
- Preserve an accessible text label; icons never carry the meaning alone.

## Affiliation and experience marks

The experience strip is biography, not customer proof.

- Approved language: `Built by the people from`.
- Required qualifier: `Individual prior experience—not customers, partners or endorsements.`
- Never use `Trusted by`, customer language, or an unqualified `Built by` beside these marks.
- Render all marks in one monochrome ivory-grey treatment on equal translucent blocks so the Traceli field remains visible. Keep sufficient mark opacity for legibility; Traceli remains the only full brand expression in the section.
- Keep the marks optically balanced rather than forcing identical pixel dimensions.
- Verify the named person's genuine relationship before adding a mark.
- Record written mark-use approval where an organization's policy requires it. If affiliation or permission is unresolved, remove the mark and use plain text until cleared.

The initial candidate set is WFP, UN Climate Change, Oxfam, Google, and Careem. Inclusion in code is not evidence that the relationship or permission gate has passed. Track the gate in [AFFILIATION_REGISTER.md](./AFFILIATION_REGISTER.md); do not put private employment evidence or personal records in the public repository.

## Demonstration and evidence rules

- Use an actual video file for the public walkthrough, not a timed carousel presented as video.
- Cache DIHAD demonstration assets so playback does not depend on a third-party portal.
- Use a public completed tender or a buyer-approved irreversibly sanitized pack.
- Fictional suppliers, awards, integrity concerns, and decisions must be labelled fictional.
- Do not imply that a staged import, integration, risk signal, or audit export is live when it is not.
- Every consequential product claim should eventually open its source, actor, and chronology.

## Public consequence evidence and case studies

Public failure examples are category evidence, not customer stories. Every published example must:

- Use the issuing institution's original audit, investigation, sanctions notice or official decision as the primary source.
- Distinguish an allegation, audit observation, investigative finding, recommendation, final decision and sanction. Do not collapse these into the word `corruption`.
- Preserve the source's dates, amounts, actors, geography and procedural status precisely.
- State the control or evidence failure and the institutional consequence separately.
- Avoid saying Traceli would have prevented the event. At most, explain which proof or control question the Traceli model is designed to surface earlier.
- Avoid implying that the named institution is a Traceli customer, prospect, partner or endorser.
- Pass a factual review and a legal/comms review before moving from the research register to public marketing.
- Carry an owner, source URL, retrieval date, claim status and review date in [PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md](./PUBLIC_CASE_STUDY_REGISTER.md).

Prefer four consequence families so the category remains broad but coherent:

1. Auditability — the record was incomplete or the process could not be adequately assessed.
2. Financial eligibility — payments or expenditure were considered ineligible or recommended for recovery.
3. Funding continuity — future funding was held behind remediation or stronger controls.
4. Integrity exposure — false statements, rigging, conflicts or other sanctionable practices led to discipline or debarment.

## Contact placement

The canonical public contact address is `hello@trace-li.com`.

- Place it beneath the final pilot invitation and in the global footer, where contact intent is highest.
- Keep it out of the first viewport so the ten-second promise and primary walkthrough action remain dominant.
- Use a direct `mailto:` link and display the full address.
- Do not publish an alternate spelling or mailbox domain without confirming that its MX records and delivery path are active.

## Release gates

Before a public brand release:

1. The ten-second promise and ninety-second journey still hold at desktop and mobile widths.
2. The Traceli lockup, product glyph grammar, palette, and typography match this reference.
3. Affiliation wording is present and every displayed mark has an owner, verified relationship, and recorded permission status.
4. The product film returns `200`, supports byte ranges, plays from a clean state, and retains its fictional-data disclosure.
5. Keyboard focus, contrast, reduced motion, alt text, and responsive overflow checks pass.
6. Any deliberate exception is documented in the implementation plan before release.
7. Every public failure example is present in the case-study register with a primary source and approved claim language.
8. Every quantified value claim identifies whether it is measured, customer-provided, assumed, or sourced; no scenario output is presented as a Traceli benchmark.
